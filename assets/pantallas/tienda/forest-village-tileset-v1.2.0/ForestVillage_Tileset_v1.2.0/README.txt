FOREST VILLAGE - 16x16 TOP-DOWN TILESET  v1.2.0
=========================================================

An original SNES-era (16-bit) style top-down tileset with a forest village theme.
Every pixel was generated by original code and hand-designed pixel patterns. There is no third-party art in this pack and no characters.

CONTENTS AT A GLANCE
--------------------
- 3 blob autotile sets x 47 tiles x 3 edge variants: dirt, sandy path, cobblestone plaza with crumbled curb
  Edges are irregular and variants join seamlessly, so roads and plazas do not repeat every 16 px.
- Water: 15 corner-matched shore pieces x 3 variants with true diagonals, half steps and round corners (the shore crosses a tile at any
  8 px offset, so there are no 16 px stairs). Dark shallow band and animated foam line (4 frames).
- 16 grass variants (calm, tufts, sunny and shaded patches, flowers, clover) with painting weights
- Path centres: pebbles and cart tracks. Overlays: cart tracks, pebbles, puddle. Sand<->cobble transition overlays.
- Fence autotile (16 pieces) with post caps and ground shadow, 6 gates: 2-post east-west, north-south, small garden wicket (each open and closed)
- Bridges: 2 tiles wide (north-south) or 2 tiles tall (east-west), with stone abutments on both banks, railings, posts, plank
  variants and a shadow on the water. 2 plank floors.
- 6 houses: thatched log cabin (48x48), red-roof cottage (64x64), blue-roof townhouse (48x64), blacksmith with forge (80x64),
  inn with dormers and hanging sign (96x80), red barn (80x80)
- Modular house kit (65 pieces): roofs in red, blue, green and slate, plaster, timber, log and stone walls with windows, doors and corners,
  dormers, chimney, foundation, doorstep, shutters, flower box, lantern
- 9 trees and wood props: large and small oak, autumn oak, birch, large and small pine, stump, log pile, fallen log
- 8 bushes and ground plants: small, large, berry and two flowering bushes, hedge segment, fern, tall grass
- 7 rocks: small, medium, mossy, two boulders, cluster, stepping stones
- 8 flower props (7 patches + a raised flower bed), sunflower, mushrooms, reeds, lily pads, pebbles
- 21 market props: 3 striped stalls (red, blue, green), hand cart, 6 crates (plain, stacked, apples, cabbages, carrots, fish),
  4 barrels (plain, water, apples, pair), 2 sacks, 2 baskets, bucket, 2 hay props
- 7 signs: signpost, small sign, notice board, 4 hanging shop signs (inn, bakery, potions, smith)
- 5 village props: stone well, lamp post, bench, water trough, campfire

PROP RULES
----------
- The bottom edge of every prop image is the ground line. A soft shadow is baked into the lower 2-4 px. All props use the same shadow rule:
  light from the top-left, shadow falls to the bottom-right.
- Props sit on the 16px grid, so a 48x64 house covers 3x4 tiles. Place its bottom-left cell where you want it to stand.
- Y-sort props by their bottom edge (minus ~3 px). In the Godot TileSet this is already set via y_sort_origin.

GODOT 4 QUICK START (tested with Godot 4.3)
-------------------------------------------
1. Copy the folder  godot/forest_village  into your project root, so you get res://forest_village/forest_village_tileset.tres
2. Pixel-art projects should use nearest filtering:
   Project Settings > Rendering > Textures > Default Texture Filter = Nearest.
3. Create a TileMapLayer, assign forest_village_tileset.tres as its Tile Set.
4. Use TWO layers for the ground:
   - Layer "Ground": open the Terrains tab, choose the terrain set "Ground" and paint Grass over the whole area (variants are picked at
     random by weight), then paint Water for ponds and rivers. Shore pieces (round corners, diagonals, half steps) are chosen for
     you. Water extends half a tile past the cells you paint, so a one-cell-wide stroke gives a two-tile-wide river.
   - Layer "Terrain": paint Dirt, Path or Cobblestone (47-tile blob sets with 3 random edge variants). Empty cells show the ground layer.
   Keep a tile of grass between water and sand. Path and Cobblestone are separate terrains: where they meet, place the sand/cobble
   transition overlay tiles (fv_terrain.png, bottom right) on a layer above, or leave a one-tile grass gap.
5. Fences: use the terrain set "Wooden fence" (match sides) on its own layer. Paint a line and posts and rails connect. Gates and
   bridges are plain tiles in the structures source. A bridge is 3 rows long: end piece on the bank, middle piece(s) over the water, end piece.
6. Props: the TileSet has an "objects" source with every prop as a tile. Large props are multi-cell tiles; they carry
   collision shapes (blocking props only) and a y_sort_origin. Put them on their own layer and enable Y Sort on it.
   Anchor rule: a prop's anchor is its BOTTOM-LEFT cell, the cell it stands on. Paint a 3x4-cell house on the cell where its
   door row should be and it grows up and to the right. For pixel-exact placement use the PNGs in props/ as Sprite2D textures instead.
7. House kit: the "house kit" source holds the modular roof, wall and detail pieces as plain tiles for building your own houses.
8. Water is animated (source "fv_water_anim"): 4 frames, 0.35 s each. Change the frame duration in the TileSet editor if you like.
9. Open godot/forest_village/demo/demo_village.tscn (Godot 4.3+) and press F6 to see the sample village built from these assets.

OTHER ENGINES (Tiled, Unity, GameMaker, Phaser, ...)
----------------------------------------------------
Slice the PNGs on a 16x16 grid. atlas_index.json lists every tile.
Autotile blocks use the common 47-tile "blob" mask:
  bit values N=1 NE=2 E=4 SE=8 S=16 SW=32 W=64 NW=128, a corner bit only counts when both neighbouring sides are set.
Inside each 8x6 block the 47 tiles are ordered by ascending mask value, left to right, top to bottom (slot 48 is a spare fill variant).
The three edge variants of a set are stacked vertically. Pick one at random per cell; they all join seamlessly.
Water: 4-bit corner patterns (TL=1, TR=2, BR=4, BL=8 = tile corner is water); tile of a cell = pattern of its four corners.
Water animation: fv_water_anim.png has one row per piece (variant 0 patterns 1-15, then variant 1, then 2), 4 frames across.
